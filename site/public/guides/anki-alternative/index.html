Anki, Quizlet or Chompbook: which to choose for remembering

Updated 10 November 2026

Anki and Quizlet are the best-known apps for memorising. Chompbook tackles a similar problem differently. Here's an honest comparison: who each one suits and when to combine them.

At a glance

Anki

Anki is a free, open-source desktop program; on Android there's the free AnkiDroid, on iPhone the paid AnkiMobile app. Its strengths are powerful spaced repetition (including the modern FSRS algorithm) and flexibility: you can make a card for anything.

The downsides: you create cards yourself, which takes time; the interface is unfamiliar to beginners; cards test short facts well but understanding of long texts poorly.

Quizlet

Quizlet is a flashcard-set service with study modes, tests and games, plus a huge catalogue of other users' sets. There's a free version; some modes are in the paid subscription. It's good for vocabulary, terms and school test prep.

The downsides are similar: a "question — short answer" format, and others' sets sometimes contain mistakes.

Chompbook

Chompbook doesn't ask you to make cards. You add a book or PDF and the app cuts it into lessons sized to your time and takes you through them in order. Each lesson ends with an open question: you answer in your own words, and AI checks whether you understood, scores it and points out what you missed. Review is built in: mistakes and gaps come back later.

Honest limits: Android only for now, an iPhone version is planned; for drilling large vocabularies or characters, Anki's cards are more convenient.

Which to choose

  • Learning words, terms, anatomy, dates — Anki or Quizlet.
  • Want to finish and understand a book, textbook or documentation — Chompbook.
  • Need both — combine them: understanding through lessons, separate facts through cards.

You can start with Chompbook's free courses, such as "Critical Thinking" or "Financial Literacy".

FAQ

Can I import Anki decks into Chompbook?

No, Chompbook works with texts: books, PDFs, notes and web pages. Your decks stay in Anki.

Is Chompbook a free alternative to Anki?

Chompbook is free, but it's less a replacement than a different approach: lessons from your material with a check of understanding instead of flashcards.

Is Chompbook on iPhone?

Not yet — it's available for Android. An iPhone version is planned.

Try it on your own material

Chompbook turns your books, PDFs and notes into short lessons with quizzes and an open question checked by AI. Free, no accounts or ads.
