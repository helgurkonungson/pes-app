Spaced repetition: what it is and how to use it

Updated 10 November 2026

Spaced repetition is one of the best-studied ways to remember for the long term. The idea is simple: come back to the material not in a row but with growing gaps, just as it starts to fade.

What spaced repetition is

Instead of reviewing something five times in one evening, you review it five times over a month — for example after 1 day, 3 days, a week, two weeks and a month. If recall was easy, the next gap is longer. If you got it wrong, it comes back sooner.

Why it works

This is the spacing effect, one of the most robust findings in memory research, replicated in hundreds of experiments (a large review: Cepeda and colleagues, 2006). Recalling something that has started to fade takes effort, and that effort makes the memory stronger. Reviewing while it's fresh adds almost nothing: you just recognise what you saw a minute ago.

Which intervals to use

  • To start: 1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 14 days → 30 days.
  • Got it wrong? Bring it back the next day and restart the chain.
  • Exact numbers matter less than the principle: gaps grow, mistakes come back earlier.

Apps calculate intervals for you. The classic algorithm is SM-2 from SuperMemo, long used by Anki; a newer one, FSRS, adapts to your own memory.

Flashcards or lessons

Flashcards are great for facts: words, dates, terms, formulas. For books and complex topics they fall short: you can remember a definition and still not know how to apply it. Understanding needs reviewing ideas and how they connect — explaining in your own words, solving problems, answering "why".

How review works in Chompbook

In Chompbook you don't make cards. The app builds lessons from your material, and review is built into studying:

  • questions you got wrong come back later;
  • every fifth lesson starts with a check of what you've covered;
  • AI remembers what you missed in your open answer and brings it back in later lessons until you've got it.

Common mistakes

  • Cramming the night before. It gets you through tomorrow's test, but a week later most of it is gone.
  • Reviewing only what you already know. Pleasant but useless — time should go to the hard parts.
  • Taking on too much new material. Reviews pile up; less new material, but every day, works better.

Review principles are also covered in the free course "How to Learn Effectively".

FAQ

How many reviews does it take to remember forever?

Usually 4–6 successful reviews with growing gaps give memory for months. The exact number depends on how hard and how familiar the material is.

How is spaced repetition different from cramming?

Cramming is many reviews in a short time. Spaced repetition is the same effort spread over days, and it gives much longer-lasting memory.

Do I need a special app?

You can keep a schedule in a notebook, but an app calculates intervals and won't let you skip a review. Anki is handy for flashcards, Chompbook for lessons from books and PDFs.
